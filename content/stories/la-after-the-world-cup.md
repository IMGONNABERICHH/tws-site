A just hosted a World Cup. Eight matches. 560,000 people through the gates. Fan zones, community events, the whole thing. By the numbers they're pushing, it went well.

But the real question is what happens after.

LA doesn't have time to rest. The Super Bowl lands in February 2027. Then the Olympics in 2028. Three massive events in three years. The same stadium that just hosted a World Cup quarter-final becomes a swimming pool for the Games. That's the schedule.

Most cities get years to figure out what they're doing with a World Cup. LA gets seven months.

Here's the smart part: LA built this with temporary infrastructure. No new stadium. No white elephant sitting around taking money. That's the right call. It means you can't point to a building and call it legacy. What actually stays is harder to see.

It's kids with access to fields. It's coaching infrastructure that might stick around. It's clinics that ran during the tournament and might still exist in November when nobody's watching. Those things compound or they don't. That depends on execution, not on events.

The real work isn't inside the stadiums during the tournament. It's between events. It's the Coliseum when there's no match. It's the blocks around SoFi in the quiet months. It's whether pickup games on public fields still happen in 2029 when the circus moves to Brisbane.

The tournament is the easy part to photograph. The city that remains after is the actual test.
