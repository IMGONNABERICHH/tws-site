The tenth FIFA Women's World Cup runs June 24 to July 25, 2027 in Brazil. Thirty-two teams. Eight cities. Sixty-four matches.

This matters more than it looks.

Here's why: it's the last 32-team tournament before FIFA expands to 48. Whatever happens in Brazil becomes the final argument for this format. Everyone debating whether the women's game is competitive is about to get their data set. Then it changes.

Brazil hasn't hosted a Women's World Cup before. The federation only formalized a professional women's league recently. This tournament is the biggest forcing function their infrastructure has ever faced. Either the game gets investment now or it doesn't.

Spain comes in as defending champions. They won in 2023 and took the men's title in July. No country has held both titles at the same time. That hasn't happened before.

The venues are all existing stadiums from the 2014 men's tournament. Belo Horizonte, Brasília, Fortaleza, Porto Alegre, Recife, Rio, Salvador, São Paulo. Brazil built it on infrastructure that was already there. No new stadiums. Just execution.

The actual logistics matter: Brazil is massive, the eight cities are spread across five regions. If you're going, book your flights and accommodation early. Group stage looks fine on a map until you realize it's four-hour flights between matches.

The format is standard eight groups of four, top two advance. Spain and Brazil qualify automatically. The rest sort through qualifiers that haven't finished resolving yet.
